=== intro ===
When a variable can only take separate values, its whole behaviour fits in a table. From that table come the expectation and the variance.


=== syllabus ===
- Draw up a probability distribution table for a discrete random variable
- Use the fact that the probabilities in a distribution sum to 1
- Calculate and use the expectation E(X) and variance Var(X) of a discrete random variable
- Use the binomial and geometric distributions as models, and know their conditions
- Calculate probabilities, means and variances for the binomial and geometric distributions

=== mistakes ===
- Forgetting to check that the probabilities sum to 1, which is often how an unknown is found.
- Using Var(X) = E(X²) without subtracting [E(X)]².
- Applying the binomial distribution where trials are not independent or p is not constant.
- Miscounting 'at least one' — it is usually quicker as 1 − P(none).
